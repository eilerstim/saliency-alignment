Having established \emph{that} alignment reshapes attention, we now ask \emph{where} in the model it happens and \emph{where it breaks}.

\subsection{The language model, not the projector, absorbs the signal}
\label{sec:analysis-where}
A natural hypothesis is that supervising attention should reshape the multimodal projector---the interface that decides how image content enters the decoder. The ablation in \Cref{tab:align} says otherwise: training the language model alone recovers the full gain, while training the projector alone lags. To see why, we measure parameter \emph{drift} between each fine-tuned checkpoint and the base model, reporting the relative update $\lVert \Delta\theta \rVert / \lVert \theta \rVert$ per component.

Two observations emerge. First, when both components are trainable, the projector's relative update is far smaller than the language model's within the same run (proj/LM ratio $\approx$ \tbd, i.e.\ $\ll 1$): given the choice, the gradient flows into the decoder and leaves the projector almost untouched.\todo{Insert the proj/LM relative-update ratio from \texttt{compare\_drift.py}.} Second, the language model's update under \emph{both}-trainable and \emph{LM-only} training is comparable and concentrated in the same place (next section), indicating the decoder, not the projector, is the natural locus of saliency adaptation. This is consistent with the view that the projector fixes a static image-to-token mapping early in pretraining, whereas the token-conditioned routing that determines \emph{where a given word looks} is computed by the decoder's attention.

\subsection{Adaptation concentrates in a few attention heads}
\label{sec:analysis-heads}
If the decoder absorbs the signal, does it spread the change across all of attention or concentrate it? Slicing the language model's attention-projection drift by layer and by head, we find adaptation is highly \emph{concentrated} rather than uniform: a small number of layers and heads account for most of the change (Gini coefficient across heads $\approx\,$\tbd, far from the $0$ of a uniform spread). Moreover, the top-drifting heads are largely \emph{shared} between the LM-only and both-trainable runs (top-$k$ Jaccard overlap $\approx\,$\tbd), so the same heads are recruited regardless of whether the projector is also free.\todo{Insert the across-head Gini and the cross-run top-$k$ Jaccard from \texttt{compare\_drift.py --per-head}.}

This dovetails with the localization-head hypothesis~\citep{kang2025your, kim2025interpreting}: only a few heads do the grounding work, and they yield the most interpretable maps. Our objective does not appear to teach the model a new grounding mechanism so much as \emph{sharpen the one it already has}, preferentially updating its grounding-relevant heads. It also explains the data efficiency of the method---$200$ steps suffice precisely because the update is low-dimensional, touching a small set of heads rather than the whole decoder.

\subsection{A failure mode on background ``stuff''}
\label{sec:analysis-stuff}
The one place alignment hurts is broad, amorphous background---``stuff'' classes such as snow and sky (\Cref{fig:qual}, bottom). After fine-tuning, the base model's sensible spreading of saliency across a snowy ground is replaced by a withdrawal of mass onto foreground objects. We attribute this to the \emph{supervision distribution} rather than the objective itself. Grounded captions reference discrete, nameable entities---individual objects and object groups---and almost never attach a token to a large background region. The alignment target $\mathbf{T}_t$ therefore practically never places mass on stuff, and because the cross-entropy still rewards \emph{mentioning} the background while the alignment term only ever pulls mass toward objects, the model learns to treat ``unannotated'' as ``unattended.'' The control is telling: background entities that \emph{are} annotated because they have definite extent (plants, branches) stay well localized---it is specifically the un-annotatable, region-filling categories that degrade.

The fix follows directly from the diagnosis. COCONut provides full \emph{panoptic} masks, including stuff segments; extending the grounding signal to attach stuff tokens to their panoptic regions---or, more simply, exempting background tokens from the alignment term so cross-entropy alone governs them---would remove the implicit ``attend only to objects'' pressure. We leave a careful study of stuff-aware supervision to future work.
