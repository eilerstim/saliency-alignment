We introduced the Saliency Alignment Loss, a parameter-free, architecture-agnostic auxiliary objective that supervises a VLM's token-conditioned attention with region-level grounding annotations during fine-tuning. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, only $200$ steps on grounded COCONut-PanCap captions measurably sharpen attention localization across three intrinsic metrics---chance-normalized Attention Mass Ratio, pixel-level Average Precision, and Normalized Scanpath Saliency---while leaving language modeling quality intact. Qualitatively, saliency maps become cleaner: mass that the base model scattered onto image borders and background is redrawn onto the objects a token names.

Beyond the headline effect, our analysis offers a mechanistic account of \emph{where} the model adapts. The language model, not the multimodal projector, absorbs the alignment gradient, and it does so through a small, run-consistent set of attention heads---evidence that the objective sharpens an existing grounding mechanism rather than installing a new one, and an explanation for its data efficiency. We also identified a clean limitation: because grounded captions annotate discrete entities and rarely broad backgrounds, alignment withdraws attention from ``stuff'' such as snow and sky, a failure that points directly at stuff-aware supervision as the remedy.

Two directions follow. First, closing the background gap by extending the grounding signal to panoptic stuff segments or by exempting background tokens from the alignment term. Second, connecting sharper intrinsic localization to downstream grounding and counting performance---which will require evaluation suites that do not overlap COCO, the contamination that motivated our intrinsic protocol in the first place. We hope that treating attention as a directly supervisable signal, rather than only a diagnostic one, proves a useful lever for building VLMs that look where they should.
