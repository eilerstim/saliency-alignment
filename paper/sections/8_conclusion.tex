We introduced the Saliency Alignment Loss, a parameter-free, architecture-agnostic auxiliary objective that supervises a VLM's token-conditioned attention with region-level grounding annotations. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, only $200$ steps on grounded COCONut-PanCap captions transform attention localization (mean Attention Mass Ratio rises $4.6{\times}$, Average Precision doubles, and Normalized Scanpath Saliency rises sevenfold) while leaving language modeling intact, and the qualitative maps confirm it: mass the base model scattered onto image borders and background is redrawn onto the objects a token names.

Our analysis pinpoints \emph{where} this happens: the language model, not the multimodal projector, absorbs the signal, identically whether or not the projector is trainable, reaching a reproducible solution concentrated in a small set of attention heads (\Cref{app:drift}). This echoes the localization-head hypothesis, now induced by explicit supervision, and helps explain the method's data efficiency.

We are also clear about what the present recipe does \emph{not} yet do: the large gains in attention quality do not translate into higher downstream benchmark scores, so saliency-map quality and task grounding are, for now, dissociated. We read this not as a defect of the objective but as evidence that localization is a capability worth supervising in its own right. We also identified a clean failure mode (alignment withdraws attention from background ``stuff'' the annotations never cover) and its direct fix in stuff-aware supervision.

These findings set up the work in progress: porting the objective into the standard LLaVA-1.5 instruction-tuning recipe with LoRA, to retain the intrinsic gains while preserving general capabilities and to test whether decoupling the signal from a caption-only diet closes the transfer gap. We hope that treating attention as a directly supervisable signal, rather than only a diagnostic one, proves a useful lever for building VLMs that look where they should.
