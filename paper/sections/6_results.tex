\Cref{tab:align} reports the three localization metrics on held-out images, and the effect is large. The base model already places above-chance mass inside annotated regions ($\mathrm{AMR}=1.25$), confirming that grounding-relevant attention exists, but the alignment loss transforms it: training the language model raises mean AMR to $5.8$, a $4.6{\times}$ increase and still $4{\times}$ on the outlier-robust median ($1.19 \!\to\! 4.70$), while AP more than doubles ($0.22 \!\to\! 0.55$) and NSS rises sevenfold ($0.26 \!\to\! 1.92$). All three move together: AMR confirms more mass lands in the mask, AP confirms in-mask pixels are genuinely ranked higher rather than the map being uniformly inflated, and NSS puts the contrast at nearly two standard deviations above the image mean: exactly the gains the objective targets, achieved in a short fine-tune without disturbing the model's other behavior. \Cref{sec:results-bench} asks whether they also help downstream.

The ablation is unambiguous about \emph{where} this happens. Plain caption fine-tuning without the loss (Caption SFT) barely moves the metrics ($\mathrm{AMR}\,1.46$), and aligning the projector alone is little better ($1.51$): the projector simply cannot reshape token-conditioned localization much. Aligning the language model is what matters, and adding the projector on top of the LM is essentially a no-op (LM $5.76$ vs.\ LM+proj.\ $5.90$, within their large spread). The two LM-trainable variants are statistically indistinguishable here, a behavioral equivalence we trace to the optimization geometry in \Cref{sec:analysis-where}. The high AMR variance ($\mathrm{std}\approx 4.6$) reflects the metric's sensitivity to small masks, which is why we report the median alongside; the median tells the same story.

A method that reshapes attention this dramatically should, one hopes, help on tasks that need grounding. It does not, at least not yet. \Cref{tab:bench} shows the LM-aligned models tracking the base model across the suite: small gains on spatial reasoning (CV-Bench, MMStar) are offset by small drops elsewhere (counting, MMVP, POPE), and no variant is consistently ahead. The per-benchmark differences among base, caption SFT, and the LM-aligned models are at the $1$--$2$ point level, within run-to-run noise that we did not characterize with multiple seeds; we therefore read \Cref{tab:bench} for the \emph{absence} of a consistent gain rather than for any single number. General VQA and hallucination are likewise preserved by LM-alignment (GQA and POPE; \Cref{app:breakdown}). The projector-only model is the exception, and an instructive one: it is the only variant whose downstream profile changes sharply, lowering the bias rate ($41.7\!\to\!35.9$) but degrading hallucination (POPE $84.3\!\to\!73.2$) and counting, a capability-for-behavior trade we explain in \Cref{sec:analysis-pheno}.

This gap between large intrinsic gains and flat downstream numbers is the central tension of the direct-fine-tuning regime, and it has two plausible causes: training on dense captions may push the model toward a captioning style that the benchmarks do not reward, and sharper attention may simply not be what bottlenecks these tasks for a 7B model. The instruction-tuning regime of \Cref{sec:regimes} targets the first cause directly; we return to it in \Cref{sec:results-it}.

\Cref{fig:qual} makes the metrics tangible through two representative cases; it is meant to illustrate the effect, not to quantify it (the systematic version is \Cref{tab:align}). First, alignment \emph{cleans up} the maps: the base model frequently deposits bright spots on non-informative regions (image edges and, characteristically, the bottom border) and distributes mass unevenly across the objects a token names, whereas after alignment these spurious peaks are suppressed and the mass redistributes onto the referent more uniformly (the flying birds), exactly the mass-covering behavior \Cref{eq:align} induces.

Second, the same pressure that concentrates attention on discrete objects can \emph{over}-concentrate at the expense of broad backgrounds: for a token like \texttt{snow}, the aligned model withdraws the saliency the base model sensibly spread across the snowy ground, whereas background \emph{objects} with definite extent (plants, branches) stay well localized. We take up this asymmetry, and its root in the annotation distribution, in \Cref{sec:analysis-stuff}.

\subsection{Toward downstream transfer: instruction tuning}
\label{sec:results-it}
The instruction-tuned regime of \Cref{sec:regimes} targets the first cause directly: porting the objective into the standard LLaVA-1.5 665K mixture via LoRA lets the grounded captions enter as one part of a diverse instruction diet rather than the entire training signal, aiming to retain the localization gains of \Cref{tab:align} without over-fitting to a captioning style. These experiments are ongoing.
