\subsection{Dataset and grounding signal}
We draw our grounding signal from COCONut-PanCap~\citep{deng2025coconut}, which pairs COCO images~\citep{lin2015microsoftcococommonobjects} with high-quality panoptic masks and human-verified, detailed captions. Crucially, each caption is \emph{grounded}: mentioned entities are tagged with the panoptic segment(s) they refer to. We parse these tags to (i) recover the clean caption shown to the model and (ii) assign every caption token the segment id(s) it references; the per-token mask $\mathbf{M}_t$ of \Cref{sec:method} is then the union of the referenced segments in the panoptic mask. Relative to grounded-caption corpora such as GranD~\citep{rasheed2024glammpixelgroundinglarge} or PNG~\citep{gonzalez2021panopticnarrativegrounding}, COCONut-PanCap provides substantially denser annotations, yielding more supervised tokens per image. We hold out the last $10{,}000$ annotated images for validation with a deterministic split and reuse it at evaluation, so all intrinsic metrics are computed on images the model never saw.

\subsection{Training regimes}
\label{sec:regimes}
We study the objective under two recipes. The first is the focus of our analysis and is fully evaluated below; the second is an ongoing effort to convert intrinsic gains into downstream ones.

\textbf{Direct fine-tuning.} We fine-tune LLaVA-1.5-7B on grounded COCONut captions with the prompt \emph{``Describe the image in detail.''} and the clean caption as target, using the objective of \Cref{eq:total} at $\lambda=0.5$. The vision encoder is always frozen; since the term adds no parameters, the only structural choice is which component receives the gradient, and we compare the multimodal \emph{projector} only, the \emph{language model} only, and \emph{both}. As a control we also run plain supervised fine-tuning with no alignment term ($\lambda=0$), which isolates the effect of training on dense captions from the effect of the loss. Full training details are deferred to \Cref{app:training}. Saliency is extracted on the fly with an eager-attention backend.\footnote{We release our saliency-extraction code as the \texttt{vl-saliency} package: \url{https://pypi.org/project/vl-saliency/}.}

\textbf{Instruction tuning (in progress).} Direct fine-tuning on dense captions risks over-optimizing the model toward captioning rather than general grounding. We therefore port the objective into the standard visual-instruction-tuning recipe, which lets us inject our caption-dense, grounded data \emph{without} drowning the model in caption-heavy text. Concretely, starting from the LLaVA-1.5 stage-1 checkpoint (pretrained projector, not yet instruction-tuned), we instruction-tune on the standard LLaVA-1.5 665K instruction mixture, replacing only the detailed-description answers with grounded COCONut-PanCap captions; the rest of the mixture (conversation, reasoning, and academic VQA) is untouched, so the alignment loss applies to the grounded subset while the remainder preserves general instruction following. We use LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} adapters on the language model's attention and MLP projections (with the projector trained directly), rather than full fine-tuning, to mitigate catastrophic forgetting of base capabilities: LoRA can only amplify directions already present in the model, which is well matched to our finding that the alignment adaptation is reproducible and concentrated in a stable set of attention heads (\Cref{app:drift}). Results for this regime are forthcoming (\Cref{sec:results-it}).

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:metrics}
We evaluate on two axes. Because the objective is designed to change \emph{where} the model attends, we first measure attention localization directly, with three complementary \emph{intrinsic} metrics on a held-out COCONut split we fully control; we then test whether this sharper attention carries over to a broad \emph{downstream} suite. Some downstream benchmarks draw on COCO images and so may overlap our training data; we mark these with $\star$ in \Cref{tab:bench} to flag this potential contamination.

\textbf{Intrinsic metrics.} For each grounded token we measure how well its saliency localizes to its mask, using three complementary metrics, each computed per supervised token, averaged over an image's tokens, then over images.
\emph{Attention Mass Ratio (AMR)}, chance-normalized, is the fraction of saliency mass inside the mask divided by the chance level $|\mathbf{M}_t|/HW$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AMR}_t
= \frac{\sum_{ij}\mathbf{S}_{t,ij}\,\mathbf{M}_{t,ij}\big/\sum_{ij}\mathbf{S}_{t,ij}}
       {|\mathbf{M}_t|/HW},
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{AMR}=1$ is chance, ${>}1$ concentrates inside the mask; it is the test-time analog of our training target.
\emph{Average Precision (AP)} is the area under the pixel-level precision--recall curve, scoring pixels by saliency $\mathbf{S}_t$ against the binary label $\mathbf{M}_t$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AP}_t &= \int_0^1 P_t(r)\,\mathrm{d}r, \\
P_t(\tau) &= \frac{\sum_{ij}\mathbf{M}_{t,ij}\,\mathbf{1}[\mathbf{S}_{t,ij}\ge\tau]}
                  {\sum_{ij}\mathbf{1}[\mathbf{S}_{t,ij}\ge\tau]},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with recall $r=R_t(\tau)=\tfrac{1}{|\mathbf{M}_t|}\sum_{ij}\mathbf{M}_{t,ij}\,\mathbf{1}[\mathbf{S}_{t,ij}\ge\tau]$ swept by the threshold $\tau$; unlike AMR it rewards correctly \emph{ranking} in-mask pixels above out-of-mask ones.
\emph{Normalized Scanpath Saliency (NSS)} standardizes the map to zero mean and unit variance and averages it inside the mask,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NSS}_t = \frac{1}{|\mathbf{M}_t|}\sum_{ij}\mathbf{M}_{t,ij}\,\frac{\mathbf{S}_{t,ij}-\mu_t}{\sigma_t},
\end{equation}
where $\mu_t$ and $\sigma_t$ are the mean and standard deviation of $\mathbf{S}_t$ over the $HW$ pixels.

\textbf{Downstream suite.} Intrinsic metrics say whether attention moved; the downstream suite says whether it \emph{mattered}. We assemble a broad battery of public VLM benchmarks spanning the capabilities sharper grounding should most plausibly help---counting, 2D/3D spatial reasoning, general and fine-grained multimodal understanding, salient-object perception, and object hallucination---plus a blindness/bias probe for whether the model attends to the image at all. \Cref{tab:bench} reports the consolidated numbers; the full roster, citations, and per-configuration scores are deferred to \Cref{app:breakdown}. We also track validation cross-entropy and next-token accuracy to confirm language modeling is preserved.
