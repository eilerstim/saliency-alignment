\subsection{Dataset and grounding signal}
We draw our grounding signal from COCONut-PanCap~\citep{deng2025coconut}, which pairs COCO images~\citep{lin2015microsoftcococommonobjects} with high-quality panoptic masks and human-verified, detailed captions. Crucially, each caption is \emph{grounded}: mentioned entities are tagged with the panoptic segment(s) they refer to, in the form \texttt{<id: text>} (or \texttt{<$id_1,\dots,id_k$: text>} for groups). We parse these tags to (i) recover the clean caption shown to the model and (ii) assign every caption token the segment id(s) it references; the per-token mask $\mathbf{M}_t$ of \Cref{sec:method} is then the union of the referenced segments in the panoptic mask. Relative to grounded-caption corpora such as GranD~\citep{rasheed2024glammpixelgroundinglarge} or PNG~\citep{gonzalez2021panopticnarrativegrounding}, COCONut-PanCap provides substantially denser annotations, yielding more supervised tokens per image. We hold out the last $10{,}000$ annotated images for validation with a deterministic split and reuse it at evaluation, so all intrinsic metrics are computed on images the model never saw.

\subsection{Training regimes}
\label{sec:regimes}
We study the objective under two recipes. The first is the focus of our analysis and is fully evaluated below; the second is an ongoing effort to convert intrinsic gains into downstream ones.

\textbf{Direct fine-tuning.} We fine-tune LLaVA-1.5-7B on grounded COCONut captions with the prompt \emph{``Describe the image in detail.''} and the clean caption as target, using the objective of \Cref{eq:total} at $\lambda=0.5$. The vision encoder is always frozen; since the term adds no parameters, the only structural choice is which component receives the gradient, and we compare the multimodal \emph{projector} only, the \emph{language model} only, and \emph{both}. As a control we also run plain supervised fine-tuning with no alignment term ($\lambda=0$), which isolates the effect of training on dense captions from the effect of the loss. Optimization uses AdamW (lr $2{\times}10^{-5}$), bf16-mixed precision, gradient checkpointing, a 3\% warmup with cosine decay, and FSDP across 4 GH200 GPUs, for $200$ steps---enough, as we show, to reshape attention. Saliency is extracted on the fly with an eager-attention backend.

\textbf{Instruction tuning (in progress).} Direct fine-tuning on dense captions risks over-optimizing the model toward captioning rather than general grounding. We therefore port the objective into the standard visual-instruction-tuning recipe, for two reasons: it is the industry-standard setup and so directly comparable to released models, and it lets us inject our caption-dense, grounded data \emph{without} drowning the model in caption-heavy text. Concretely, starting from the LLaVA-1.5 stage-1 checkpoint (pretrained projector, not yet instruction-tuned), we instruction-tune on the standard LLaVA-1.5 665K instruction mixture, replacing only the detailed-description answers with grounded COCONut-PanCap captions; the rest of the mixture (conversation, reasoning, and academic VQA) is untouched, so the alignment loss applies to the grounded subset while the remainder preserves general instruction following. We use LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} adapters on the language model's attention and MLP projections (with the projector trained directly), rather than full fine-tuning, to mitigate catastrophic forgetting of base capabilities: LoRA can only amplify directions already present in the model, which is well matched to our finding that the alignment adaptation is low-dimensional and reproducible (\Cref{sec:analysis-heads}). Results for this regime are forthcoming (\Cref{sec:results-it}).

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:metrics}
We evaluate on two axes: \emph{intrinsic} attention localization on held-out COCONut images, and \emph{downstream} task performance on a benchmark suite. Because public grounding and counting benchmarks are largely built on COCO~\citep{han2024coco}, which overlaps our training images, the intrinsic metrics---computed on a held-out split we fully control---are our primary measure; the downstream suite is chosen to limit COCO overlap.

\textbf{Intrinsic metrics.} For each grounded token we measure how well its saliency localizes to its mask, using three complementary metrics, each computed per supervised token, averaged (ignoring undefined cases) over an image's tokens, then over images.
\emph{Attention Mass Ratio (AMR)}, chance-normalized, is the fraction of saliency mass inside the mask divided by the chance level $|\mathbf{M}_t|/HW$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AMR}_t
= \frac{\sum_{ij}\mathbf{S}_{t,ij}\,\mathbf{M}_{t,ij}\big/\sum_{ij}\mathbf{S}_{t,ij}}
       {|\mathbf{M}_t|/HW},
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{AMR}=1$ is chance, ${>}1$ concentrates inside the mask; it is the test-time analog of our training target.
\emph{Average Precision (AP)} is the pixel-level area under the precision--recall curve with saliency as the score and mask membership as the label; unlike AMR it rewards correctly \emph{ranking} in-mask pixels above out-of-mask ones.
\emph{Normalized Scanpath Saliency (NSS)} is the mean $z$-score of the saliency inside the mask after standardizing the map, giving the interpretable statement ``saliency at annotated pixels is $z$ standard deviations above the image-wide mean.''

\textbf{Downstream suite.} We report counting (CountBench~\citep{paiss2023teaching}), spatial reasoning (CV-Bench 2D/3D~\citep{tong2024cambrian}), general and fine-grained multimodal ability (MMStar~\citep{chen2024mmstar}, MMVP~\citep{tong2024mmvp}), low-level salient-object perception (the O3 split of SalBench~\citep{dahou2025salbench}) and hallucination (POPE~\citep{li2023pope}), and a blindness/bias probe (VLMs-are-Blind accuracy and a bias rate~\citep{rahmanzadehgervi2024blind}). A per-configuration breakdown---including general VQA (GQA~\citep{hudson2019gqa}) and a second counting benchmark (TallyQA~\citep{acharya2019tallyqa}, FSC-147~\citep{ranjan2021counting})---is deferred to \Cref{app:breakdown}. We also track validation cross-entropy and next-token accuracy to confirm language modeling is preserved.
